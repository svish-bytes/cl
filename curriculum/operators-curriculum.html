C1

Financial literacy

Every other skill on this page needs numbers to stand on. You can’t price, choose clients or judge a bet you can’t measure.

Memorise

  1. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity, cash is reality.
  2. Profit = revenue − all costs, including a fair salary for you. If you don’t pay yourself, the profit isn’t real.
  3. Gross margin = (revenue − direct project costs) ÷ revenue. Direct costs are tool credits, freelancers, vendors and partner shares.
  4. Break-even = monthly fixed costs ÷ gross margin %.
  5. Runway = cash in bank ÷ monthly burn.
  6. GST you collect is not your money. Keep it separate the day it arrives.

Apply

  1. Build your P&L

    Last 3 months, every rupee in and out, with Elswheir and akitsuu kept separate: Higgsfield and AI subscriptions, freelancers, partner splits with AD PROPHETS and AEVORO, internet, equipment.

    Output · One-sheet P&L2 hrs

  2. Find your break-even

    Fixed monthly costs plus your minimum salary, divided by your margin. Write the number on a card above your desk.

    Output · One number30 min

  3. Set up a tax account

    Open a separate account for GST and tax. Move a fixed % of every receipt into it. Ask your CA what the % should be.

    Output · Standing instruction1 hr

Done when you can state last month’s revenue, margin, cash, receivables and break-even from memory.

C2

Unit economics

This tells you which work actually makes you money. The same lens becomes client diagnosis (B1) and production economics (A6).

Memorise

  1. Effective rate = fee ÷ actual hours. Use it to diagnose, never to price.
  2. Chase margin per project, not revenue per project.
  3. CAC is the time and money it takes to win a client. LTV is the margin earned over the whole relationship.
  4. Healthy LTV is roughly 3× CAC or more. This is a SaaS rule of thumb; use it as a rough guide.
  5. If one client is over ~40% of revenue, losing them is an existential risk (rough threshold).
  6. Revisions silently eat your margin.

Apply

  1. Review your last 5 projects

    AIME, C0C0N promo, Bodhini and two more. For each: quoted vs actual hours, tool credits, revision rounds, final margin. Rank them by margin.

    Output · Ranked table3 hrs

  2. Check your AIME dependence

    What share of the last 12 months’ revenue came from AIME? If it’s over 40%, set a 90-day target to land one more retainer.

    Output · % plus target45 min

  3. Cost per finished second

    Total production cost of your AI video pipeline ÷ seconds delivered. This is your internal floor for any quote.

    Output · Floor rate1 hr

Done when you know your three most and least profitable kinds of work, and why.

Test yourself
tomorrow

C3

Pricing & value

Value-based pricing is your stated model. This module turns it from a belief into a method you can defend in front of a client.

Memorise

  1. Price to the value of the outcome, not your effort.
  2. Value = what the result is worth in the client’s currency: enrolments, sales, price premium.
  3. Always offer three options. The middle is your target and the top sets the anchor.
  4. Never cut price without removing scope.
  5. Write your walk-away number before the call.
  6. If you win more than ~70% of pitches, you are probably underpriced (rough heuristic).

Apply

  1. Do the AIME value maths

    Fee per student × the extra enrolments a campaign could plausibly drive = the value at stake. Decide the share of it you can defend, and write down your reasoning.

    Output · Value sheet1 hr

  2. Price the Dubai client in three tiers

    Essential / Signature / Atelier, priced in AED, with the top tier as the anchor. Check the AED to INR rate on the day.

    Output · 3-tier proposal2 hrs

  3. Retire the rate card

    Rewrite your services as packages, each with scope, number of revisions, usage rights and timeline.

    Output · Package sheet2 hrs

Done when every proposal states the client’s value before your price.

Test yourself
tomorrow

Which tier do you present first, and why?

What must you remove before you lower a price?

C4

Cash & collections

Profitable studios still die of empty bank accounts. This module is mostly habit, not theory.

Memorise

  1. Work never runs ahead of payment.
  2. Take a 40 to 50% advance on project work and bill the rest by milestone.
  3. Invoice the day a milestone is hit. Follow up on fixed days.
  4. Money owed to you above one month of revenue is a red flag.
  5. Put a late-payment clause in every contract.
  6. Keep a 3 to 6 month buffer covering personal and studio costs (rough guide).

Apply

  1. List what you’re owed

    Every unpaid invoice today, with its age in days. Chase anything over 30 days this week.

    Output · Receivables list30 min

  2. Write three follow-up templates

    A friendly one for day 3, a firm one for day 15, a final notice for day 30. Write them once and reuse them.

    Output · 3 templates45 min

  3. Calculate your runway

    Cash in bank ÷ (studio plus personal monthly costs). If it’s under 3 months, that becomes priority one.

    Output · Months of runway20 min

Done when receivables stay under one month of revenue for a full quarter.

C5

Sales & negotiation

You already read people well. This module turns that into a repeatable process, and covers your partner deals.

Memorise

  1. Ask before you pitch: problem, impact, budget, decision-maker, timeline.
  2. Know your BATNA, your best alternative if the deal dies. The stronger it is, the calmer you negotiate.
  3. Trade, don’t give. Every concession should get you something back.
  4. Silence is a tool. Let the client fill it.
  5. Name the emotion: “It sounds like timing is the real concern.”
  6. For every partnership, write down what each side gives and what each side gets.

Apply

  1. Script your discovery call

    Run the five questions on the next lead and log the answers in a sales sheet with a dated next step.

    Output · Sales log rowEach call

  2. Map partner economics

    AD PROPHETS, AEVORO, and the Bodhini alliance: who brings leads, who delivers, the % splits, who owns the client relationship. Mark any imbalance.

    Output · One-page map1.5 hrs

  3. Prepare objections for Girl Rising

    Write the three objections they are most likely to raise to your producer pitch, with your answer to each.

    Output · Objection sheet45 min

Done when you never end a sales call without knowing the budget signal and who decides.

C6

Contracts, tax & compliance

Mistakes here are expensive and hard to undo. This module also covers creative usage rights (A8). Verify every point below with a CA or lawyer.

Memorise

  1. No work starts without signed scope plus advance.
  2. IP transfers only on full payment.
  3. Price usage rights separately by medium, territory and term. Cap your liability at the fee.
  4. GST: registration is generally required above ₹20 lakh aggregate turnover for services. From memory, verify.
  5. Exports such as the Dubai work may be zero-rated under a LUT if they meet the export conditions. Keep the FIRC/FIRA. Verify.
  6. Reconcile TDS against Form 26AS/AIS every quarter, or you lose the credit.

Apply

  1. Book a CA session

    Agenda: GST status, whether 44ADA applies to your work, which TDS sections clients use, the LUT for Dubai, export paperwork.

    Output · Written answers1 hr

  2. Get a master contract

    One master service agreement plus a SOW template, drafted by a lawyer. Do it once and reuse it.

    Output · TemplatesLawyer fee

  3. Find what isn’t written down

    Check the AIME and Dubai arrangements: is any scope, payment term or rights clause only verbal?

    Output · Gap list1 hr

Done when you know your quarterly tax dates and every active client has a signed agreement.

C7

Decision quality

This is the capstone, and it’s what being a “grounded but successful” businessman comes down to: deciding well on incomplete information, again and again.

Memorise

  1. Judge a decision by its process, not its outcome. Good calls can still lose.
  2. One-way doors go slow, two-way doors go fast.
  3. Assume 1.5× the cost and 2× the time. Plan cash around that.
  4. Grounded means protecting the downside. Successful means taking sized bets on the upside.
  5. Make many small, survivable bets rather than a few large ones.
  6. Write down what you expect before you know the result.

Apply

  1. Pre-mortem the backup model

    Kerala healthcare institute: “It’s 2028 and it failed. Why?” List 10 reasons, rank the top 3, and design the cheapest test for the riskiest one.

    Output · Pre-mortem sheet1.5 hrs

  2. Keep a decision journal

    Log every decision above ₹50,000 or longer than 3 months, using the template at the back. Review it monthly.

    Output · Journal10 min each

  3. Run a quarterly review

    Where did revenue come from? What should you stop, start, continue? What is the single biggest risk?

    Output · One pageHalf day

Done when you have 12 months of journal entries and can name your three most common mistakes in judgement.

Test yourself
tomorrow

Process or outcome: which do you judge a decision by?

What do you assume about cost and time?

Track C · Business acumen11

Twelve models to memorise

These are the thinking tools behind C7. Cover the right side and quiz yourself. Then name one live decision each model applies to this week.

  1. Opportunity cost

    What am I not doing if I say yes?

    A low-fee project vs time spent on the Dubai wedge.

  2. Base rates

    How does this usually go for people like me?

    A new revenue line: assume it is slower and smaller.

  3. Second-order effects

    And then what?

    A discount for one client becomes the price everyone expects.

  4. Inversion

    How would I guarantee this fails?

    Then avoid exactly that.

  5. Pre-mortem

    It’s a year later and it failed. Why?

    Run it before any big commitment.

  6. Expected value

    Probability × payoff, compared across options.

    Which pitches deserve your week.

  7. Reversibility

    Is this a one-way or a two-way door?

    Hiring, equity and long contracts are one-way doors.

  8. Margin of safety

    What if it costs 1.5× and takes 2× as long?

    Cash planning, launch timing.

  9. Concentration

    What breaks if one thing fails?

    One client, one partner, one platform.

  10. Incentives

    What does each party actually gain?

    Partner splits, client stakeholders.

  11. Sunk cost

    Would I start this today, knowing what I know?

    Long pitches going nowhere.

  12. Circle of competence

    Do I actually know this, or do I just feel confident?

    Healthcare, trading, new markets.

B1

Client business diagnosis

Most marketing problems are business problems in disguise. Find the real one before you touch a brief.

Memorise

  1. Ask: how do they make money, and where does it leak?
  2. Map the funnel: awareness → enquiry → conversion → retention → referral.
  3. Find the constraint, the one stage that limits growth. Work there.
  4. Seasonality decides timing. For NEET/JEE that means results season and admission windows.
  5. A client asking for “more reels” rarely needs more reels.

Apply

  1. AIME on one page

    Fee structure, cost per enquiry, enquiry-to-enrolment conversion, competitor fees, seasonal calendar. Circle the constraint.

    Output · One-page read3 hrs

  2. Bodhini on one page

    Channels, repeat-purchase rate, margin per bottle, who buys vs who uses. Ask AD PROPHETS for the data.

    Output · One-page read2 hrs

Done when you can explain each client’s growth constraint in one sentence.

B2

Research

Your psychology degree is a real advantage here. This is where it starts showing up in client deliverables.

Memorise

  1. Ask about past behaviour, not future intentions.
  2. Open with: “Tell me about the last time you…”
  3. 5 to 8 interviews usually surface most patterns (common qualitative practice).
  4. Keep what people say, do and feel separate.
  5. Don’t lead and don’t pitch. Watch for social-desirability bias, especially with parents.

    Output · Competitor map3 hrs

B3

Insight

This is the hinge between strategy and creative. A sharp insight makes your ideas (A1) sharper at the same time.

Memorise

  1. An observation is not an insight.
  2. Insight = a truth + a tension + the reaction “huh, that’s true”.
  3. Formula: [Audience] wants [X], but [tension], because [deeper why].
  4. Test every one: is it true, non-obvious, and something the brand can act on?
  5. The best insights live in the contradictions in your research.

Apply

  1. Write 20, keep 3

    From the AIME interviews, write 20 candidate insights in the formula. Cut to 3 and test each against the three questions.

    Output · 3 insights2 hrs

  2. Daily reverse-engineering

    For one ad you see each day, write the insight behind it in the formula. Five minutes.

    Output · Insight log5 min/day

Done when your insights make clients pause before they nod.

Positioning

Positioning is deciding what to give up. It applies to every client, and to akitsuu and Elswheir themselves.

Memorise

B5

Brand platform

The platform is the single page that keeps every future asset consistent, including work that isn’t made by you.

Memorise

  1. Purpose: only if it’s true. No purpose is better than a fake one.
